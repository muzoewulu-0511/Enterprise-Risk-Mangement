## Overview

This project presents an enterprise-wide risk assessment for **PayNest Technologies**, a fictional Nigerian fintech company providing digital payment services through web and mobile platforms.

Developed as a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) portfolio project, the assessment demonstrates how an organization can identify, evaluate, prioritize, and treat enterprise risks while establishing risk ownership, governance structures, and management reporting.

The case study addresses a common organizational challenge: business growth without a mature, integrated risk management framework.

It brings together business objectives, asset valuation, risk assessment, cybersecurity threat modelling, risk appetite, control recommendations, and established risk management frameworks to develop a structured approach to enterprise risk management.

> **Project status:** Completed case study and proposed risk management assessment. Risk ratings, tolerances, treatment plans, and residual-risk estimates are illustrative recommendations that require organizational validation.

## Business Problem Statement

PayNest Technologies is a growing fintech organization that processes digital payments, stores customer information, operates cloud infrastructure, and depends on third-party service providers.

The case study identifies several gaps in its risk management environment:

- Risk management activities are fragmented across departments.
- A comprehensive enterprise-wide risk assessment has not been established.
- Formal asset and risk registers are missing.
- Risk appetite and tolerance levels are undefined.
- Risk ownership and lines of defense are unclear.
- Risk treatment, escalation, and board reporting lack a structured approach.

These gaps can expose the organization to financial losses, customer data breaches, payment fraud, regulatory exposure, service disruption, third-party failures, and reputational damage.

### Why This Project Matters

An integrated enterprise risk management approach helps an organization connect operational and cybersecurity risks with its financial obligations, compliance responsibilities, strategic objectives, and customer expectations.

This project demonstrates a structured way to establish that connection and support informed management decisions.

## Project Objectives

The assessment aims to:

1. Establish organizational context, business objectives, critical processes, and stakeholder interests.
2. Identify and classify critical business and technology assets.
3. Identify enterprise risks across business functions.
4. Assess risks using defined likelihood, impact, and risk-rating criteria.
5. Propose risk appetite, tolerance levels, and escalation triggers.
6. Apply cybersecurity threat modelling to the customer payment platform.
7. Define risk ownership and accountability through the Three Lines Model.
8. Recommend risk treatment actions, control improvements, and accountable owners.
9. Apply ISO 31000, COSO ERM, and NIST Risk Management Framework (RMF) concepts.
10. Communicate the assessment's findings and priorities through an executive risk report.

## Key Features and Deliverables

### 1. Organizational Context and Business Objectives

Defines PayNest's operating environment, business priorities, critical processes, and stakeholder interests.

The assessment covers customer onboarding, authentication, payment processing, reconciliation, settlement, cloud operations, vendor management, customer support, and incident recovery.

### 2. Asset Register and Valuation

Develops an asset register covering critical business information, applications, infrastructure, financial resources, and operational dependencies.

Assets are evaluated using:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability
- Financial significance
- Regulatory exposure
- Operational criticality
- Reputational consequences

The report uses qualitative ratings rather than unsupported monetary valuations.

### 3. Enterprise Risk Identification

Identifies 13 risks across cybersecurity, operational, financial, compliance, third-party, strategic, reputational, workforce, and governance categories.

Each risk is connected to its potential business consequences and relevant assets.

### 4. Risk Assessment and Risk Register

Uses a consistent scoring methodology to evaluate likelihood and impact, establish risk levels, and prioritize treatment.

The risk register includes risk identifiers, descriptions, categories, likelihood scores, impact scores, overall ratings, and risk levels.

### 5. Risk Appetite and Tolerance

Proposes risk appetite statements, measurable tolerances, escalation triggers, and rationales for key risk areas.

The assessment recommends a very low appetite for material customer data exposure, unauthorized payments, material noncompliance, and failure to meet settlement obligations.

### 6. Cybersecurity Threat Modelling

Applies three complementary threat modelling approaches to PayNest's customer payment platform:

- **STRIDE:** Examines spoofing, tampering, repudiation, information disclosure, denial of service, and elevation of privilege.
- **PASTA:** Connects business objectives with application scope, threats, vulnerabilities, attack scenarios, and risk treatment.
- **Attack tree:** Illustrates the security conditions and alternative paths associated with unauthorized customer payments.

Proposed defensive measures include stronger authentication, access restrictions, transaction verification, protected audit logs, encryption, monitoring, and recovery testing.

### 7. Three Lines Model

Establishes a proposed structure for risk accountability and independent assurance.

| Line | Primary responsibility |
|---|---|
| First line | Business and operational teams own risks and operate controls. |
| Second line | Risk, compliance, privacy oversight, and security governance provide monitoring, guidance, and challenge. |
| Third line | Internal Audit provides independent assurance over governance, risk management, and controls. |

The Board retains oversight of risk appetite, accountability, and treatment progress.

### 8. Risk Treatment Plan

Recommends treatment approaches and control improvements for identified risks.

The plan covers access control, cloud security, fraud prevention, settlement liquidity, reconciliation, compliance obligations, vendor due diligence, business continuity, workforce resilience, and enterprise risk governance.

Treatment approaches include:

- Reduce risk
- Avoid risk where justified
- Transfer selected exposures
- Accept controlled residual risk where appropriate

Accountable owners and expected residual-risk ratings are proposed for treatment tracking.

### 9. Enterprise Risk Management Framework Application

Demonstrates how three established frameworks can support different aspects of the assessment:

- ISO 31000 for the overall enterprise risk management process.
- COSO ERM for integrating governance, strategy, risk, and performance.
- NIST RMF for system-level cybersecurity and privacy risk management.

### 10. Executive Risk Report

Consolidates the major findings, priority exposures, and management recommendations into an executive-level assessment.

It emphasizes accountable ownership, validated risk tolerances, funded treatment plans, monthly priority-risk reviews, and quarterly Board reporting.

## Risk Assessment Methodology

The project uses a qualitative risk assessment model based on likelihood multiplied by impact.

### Risk Scoring Formula

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

Both likelihood and impact are rated on a scale of 1 to 5.

| Score | Likelihood | Impact |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Rare | Insignificant |
| 2 | Unlikely | Minor |
| 3 | Possible | Moderate |
| 4 | Likely | Major |
| 5 | Almost certain | Severe |

### Risk Rating Scale

| Risk Score | Rating | Management response |
|---:|---|---|
| 1–4 | Low | Manage through routine controls and review. |
| 5–9 | Moderate | Assign an owner and monitor against tolerance. |
| 10–25 | High | Require a documented treatment plan and executive oversight. |

The scoring model provides a consistent basis for comparing risks and determining which exposures require management attention.

All ratings in the case study are proposed assessments rather than verified measurements of PayNest's actual operating environment.

## Priority Risks Identified

The risk register identifies the following three risks as the highest-scoring priorities, each with a proposed score of 20 out of 25.

| Risk ID | Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Score |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| R01 | Customer data breach | 4 | 5 | 20 |
| R03 | Account takeover and payment fraud | 4 | 5 | 20 |
| R05 | Liquidity or settlement shortfall | 4 | 5 | 20 |

### Recommended Treatment Priorities

**Customer data breach**
- Enforce multifactor authentication for privileged accounts.
- Review access permissions and apply least privilege.
- Validate cloud configurations and strengthen logging.
- Improve incident response and sensitive-data protection.

**Account takeover and payment fraud**
- Strengthen authentication and account recovery.
- Validate payment authorization controls.
- Introduce transaction verification and fraud monitoring.
- Establish customer remediation procedures.

**Liquidity or settlement shortfall**
- Introduce rolling cash-flow forecasts.
- Monitor settlement coverage and obligations.
- Conduct liquidity stress testing.
- Establish escalation procedures for forecast shortfalls.

The report proposes a phased implementation approach, with immediate governance and liquidity priorities, core control improvements within 90 days, and resilience testing and assurance within 180 days.

## Risk Management Frameworks

| Framework | Role in the project | Application |
|---|---|---|
| ISO 31000 | Enterprise risk management | Context, identification, analysis, evaluation, treatment, monitoring, and reporting. |
| COSO ERM | Governance and strategy | Connects risk management with objectives, performance, decision-making, and organizational oversight. |
| NIST RMF | System-level risk management | Supports system categorization, control selection, implementation, assessment, authorization, and monitoring. |

These frameworks serve complementary purposes. The project demonstrates how enterprise-wide governance and system-level security controls can support a common risk management approach.

## Key Recommendations

The assessment recommends that PayNest management:

- Establish enterprise-wide risk ownership and formal risk governance.
- Approve and maintain an enterprise risk register and asset register.
- Validate risk appetite, tolerance thresholds, and escalation requirements.
- Prioritize customer-data protection, payment fraud prevention, and settlement liquidity.
- Strengthen privileged access management, monitoring, and recovery controls.
- Maintain a register of applicable legal, regulatory, and contractual obligations.
- Improve vendor oversight, continuity planning, and incident response.
- Track treatment milestones and verify control effectiveness.
- Conduct monthly reviews of priority risks and provide quarterly reports to the Board.

These recommendations are proposed actions for the case study, not evidence that the controls have already been implemented.

This project demonstrates the ability to:

- Structure an enterprise-wide risk assessment around business objectives.

- Identify and prioritize risks using a documented scoring methodology.
- Connect cybersecurity threats with operational, financial, and customer consequences.
- Propose risk appetite statements and measurable escalation criteria.
- Define risk ownership and assurance responsibilities.

## Limitations and Assumptions

This assessment is based on a fictional fintech case study and is intended for learning and portfolio demonstration.

- Risk scores and tolerances are proposed assumptions, not validated operational measurements.
- Residual-risk ratings are estimates that require evidence of control implementation and effectiveness.
- The report does not demonstrate live security testing, a production implementation, or an independent audit.
- Applicable legal and regulatory obligations require confirmation by qualified personnel based on PayNest's actual services, operating jurisdictions, and business relationships.
- Recommendations should be validated with relevant stakeholders before being used in a real organization.
